\title{When is greedy exact for weighted minimum 3-cut?}

\author{Armando Rodado Amaris}
\ead{armando@rodadolab.com}
\affiliation{organization={Centre for Quantum Software and Information, University of Technology Sydney},
  city={Sydney},
  state={NSW},
  country={Australia}}

\begin{abstract}
The greedy algorithm for minimum 3-cut splits a global minimum-weight cut and then the cheaper of its
two sides; it is a tight $4/3$-approximation. We show that it is exact, from every global
minimum-weight cut, whenever some optimal 3-partition has two parts joined by total weight at least
the weighted edge connectivity $\lambda$. This extends both theorems of Hochbaum and Shmoys from
unweighted planar graphs to all weighted graphs, recovers the $4/3$ bound, and generalizes to minimum
$k$-cut with a one-paragraph proof. The remaining hard instances have all pair weights below
$\lambda$; for them we prove a merge lemma for Gomory--Hu sides. Finally, we give a 7-vertex graph
with a unique Gomory--Hu tree whose unique optimal 3-partition contains no side of that tree, so the
first stage of Kapoor's algorithm does not suffice on its own. The main theorem, the $4/3$ bound,
the merge lemmas and the counterexample are verified in the Lean~4 proof assistant.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Given an undirected graph $G=(V,E)$ with positive edge weights, a \emph{3-cut} is a set of edges whose
removal leaves at least three components; equivalently, the set of edges crossing a partition of $V$
into three nonempty parts. The minimum 3-cut problem asks for one of least total weight. Throughout,
``minimum'' refers to total weight, not to the number of edges (see Section~\ref{sec:prelim}).

The problem is polynomial for fixed $k$~\cite{GoldschmidtHochbaum1994}. Exact algorithms for $k=3$ have
successively used $O(n^3)$ max-flow computations~\cite{Kapoor1996,KamidoiWY2002}, an $O(mn^3)$
enumeration of light cuts~\cite{BurletGoldschmidt1997}, and $O(n)$ candidate cuts drawn from an ordered
cut enumeration~\cite{NagamochiIbaraki2000}. Vaughn~\cite{Vaughn2026} recently obtained
$O(n^2\log^2 n)$ randomized and $\tilde O(n^2)$ deterministic time; under the Max-Weight Clique
conjecture this is essentially optimal in general weighted graphs.

The simplest heuristic is the \emph{greedy algorithm}: take a global minimum-weight cut $(S,V\setminus S)$,
then a minimum-weight cut of whichever side gives the lighter result. For weighted graphs it is a tight
$4/3$-approximation~\cite{HochbaumTsai1983}, as reported in~\cite{HochbaumShmoys1985}. For unweighted
planar graphs, Hochbaum and Shmoys~\cite{HochbaumShmoys1985} proved, in the language of dual cycles, that it
is exact in two structural situations. This note asks when the greedy algorithm is exact in general.

\paragraph{Contributions.}
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*]
\item \emph{Exactness criterion} (Theorem~\ref{thm:main}). For every 3-partition $(A,B,C)$ and every
  global minimum-weight cut $S$, $\greedy(S)\le\max(\lambda+d(A),\,W(A,B,C))$. Consequently greedy is
  exact whenever some optimum has a pair of parts joined by weight at least $\lambda$. This holds in
  every weighted graph, recovers both Hochbaum--Shmoys theorems (Corollary~\ref{cor:hs}) and the $4/3$
  bound (Corollary~\ref{cor:43}), and gives near-linear exact algorithms outside a well-defined hard
  class on general, planar and bounded-genus graphs (Corollary~\ref{cor:time}). Theorem~\ref{thm:main},
  Corollaries~\ref{cor:hs} and~\ref{cor:43}, Proposition~\ref{prop:hard} and the merge lemmas are
  formalized in Lean~4.
\item \emph{Hard instances} (Section~\ref{sec:hard}). When the criterion fails, all three pair weights
  are below $\lambda$, forcing every part's cut weight into $[\lambda,2\lambda)$ and the optimum into
  $[3\lambda/2,3\lambda)$. A merge lemma shows that any Gomory--Hu side missing a vertex of each of the
  other two parts can be absorbed into a part of an optimum.
\item \emph{Gomory--Hu sides are not enough} (Section~\ref{sec:cex}). Kapoor's
  algorithm~\cite{Kapoor1996} first tries every side $X$ of a Gomory--Hu tree as a part, completing it by
  a minimum-weight cut of $G[V\setminus X]$, and then needs further recursion. We show this cannot be
  avoided: a 7-vertex graph has a unique Gomory--Hu tree and a unique minimum-weight 3-partition
  containing no Gomory--Hu side. All claims about this graph are verified in Lean~4.
\item \emph{Minimum $k$-cut} (Section~\ref{sec:kcut}). A $k$-partition is \emph{compatible} with a
  cut $S$ if each of its parts lies inside $S$ or inside $V\setminus S$. For every $k\ge3$, every
  global minimum-weight cut $S$ and every $k$-partition $P$ with parts $A_i,A_j$, the cheapest
  compatible $k$-partition weighs at most $\max(W(P)-w(A_i,A_j)+\lambda,\,W(P))$
  (Theorem~\ref{thm:kcut}). Hence every global minimum-weight cut is compatible with some
  minimum-weight $k$-partition as soon as some optimum has two parts joined by weight at least
  $\lambda$. Without this condition a global minimum-weight cut can be incompatible with every
  optimum, already for $k=3$, where this is exactly the failure of greedy. The theorem also gives a
  two-line proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:main}.
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{Relation to prior arguments.}
Two cases in the proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:main}, a minimum-weight cut crossing the lightest part and
the lightest part contained in one side, overlap Lemmas~2 and~3 of Kamidoi, Wakabayashi and
Yoshida~\cite{KamidoiWY2002}. The case where Vaughn's algorithm finishes immediately (a part of weight
below $\OPT/2$) is a special case of our criterion. The merge lemma is related to Xiao's non-crossing
lemmas for 3-way cuts in hypergraphs~\cite{Xiao2008}.

\section{Preliminaries}\label{sec:prelim}

Throughout, $G=(V,E)$ is connected with positive edge weights $w$ and $n\ge 3$ vertices. For
$X\subseteq V$, $d(X)$ is the weight of the edges with exactly one end in $X$, and
$\lambda=\min\{d(X):\emptyset\ne X\subsetneq V\}$ is the \emph{weighted edge connectivity}, the least
total weight of a cut. For disjoint $P,Q$, $w(P,Q)$ is the weight of the edges joining them. For a
3-partition $(A,B,C)$ write $w_{AB}=w(A,B)$ and so on, and
\[
  W(A,B,C) \;=\; w_{AB}+w_{AC}+w_{BC} \;=\; \tfrac12\bigl(d(A)+d(B)+d(C)\bigr).
\]
$\OPT$ is the minimum of $W$ over 3-partitions; a minimum-weight 3-partition is an \emph{optimum}. For
$U\subseteq V$ with $|U|\ge2$, $\lambda(G[U])$ is the least weight of a bipartition of the induced
subgraph into two nonempty sets ($0$ if $G[U]$ is disconnected); set $\lambda(G[U])=\infty$ if $|U|=1$.

\paragraph{Terminology.} All graphs are weighted, and ``minimum'' always refers to total edge weight,
never to the number of edges. A \emph{global minimum-weight cut} is a set $S$ with $d(S)=\lambda$; in
general it need not have the fewest edges among all cuts, and a cut with the fewest edges need not
have weight $\lambda$. The two notions agree only for unit weights, which is the setting of
Hochbaum and Shmoys~\cite{HochbaumShmoys1985}. Likewise a minimum-weight $x$--$y$ cut is a cut
separating $x$ from $y$ of least total weight $\lambda(x,y)$, and a minimum-weight 3-cut is a
3-partition of least total crossing weight.

The cut function is symmetric and submodular, hence posimodular: for all $X,Y\subseteq V$,
\[
  d(X)+d(Y)\ge d(X\cap Y)+d(X\cup Y), \qquad d(X)+d(Y)\ge d(X\setminus Y)+d(Y\setminus X).
\]

For a global minimum-weight cut $S$ (so $d(S)=\lambda$), let
\[
  \greedy(S) \;=\; \lambda + \min\bigl(\lambda(G[S]),\,\lambda(G[V\setminus S])\bigr).
\]
Splitting one side of $S$ optimally gives a 3-partition of this weight, so $\greedy(S)\ge\OPT$; greedy
is \emph{exact} if $\greedy(S)=\OPT$.

A \emph{Gomory--Hu tree}~\cite{GomoryHu1961} $T$ is a weighted tree on $V$ such that for every tree
edge $e=(x,y)$ the side $F_e$ of $T-e$ containing $x$ satisfies $d(F_e)=w_T(e)=\lambda(x,y)$, the
minimum weight of a cut separating $x$ from $y$. We call $F_e$ and $V\setminus F_e$ the
\emph{Gomory--Hu sides} of $e$. Every $Y\subseteq F_e$ with $x\in Y$ separates $x$ from $y$, so
$d(Y)\ge d(F_e)$.

\section{Greedy exactness}

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:main}
For every 3-partition $(A,B,C)$ of $V$ and every global minimum-weight cut $S$,
\[
  \greedy(S)\;\le\;\max\bigl(\lambda+d(A),\;W(A,B,C)\bigr).
\]
In particular, if some optimum $(A,B,C)$ satisfies $w_{BC}\ge\lambda$, then $\greedy(S)=\OPT$ for
every global minimum-weight cut $S$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Write $\Sbar=V\setminus S$. It suffices to exhibit a bipartition of $S$ or of $\Sbar$ into two nonempty
sets whose crossing weight is at most $d(A)$; adding $\lambda=d(S)$ then bounds $\greedy(S)$ by
$\lambda+d(A)$. The one exception is case~(ii), which yields $W(A,B,C)$ directly. Since $A\ne V$,
exactly one of the following holds, up to exchanging $S$ and $\Sbar$.
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*),leftmargin=*]
\item $A\subsetneq S$. Split $S$ into $A$ and $S\setminus A$; the crossing weight is at most $d(A)$.
\item $A=S$. Split $\Sbar$ into $B$ and $C$: $\greedy(S)\le\lambda+w_{BC}=d(A)+w_{BC}=W(A,B,C)$.
\item $S\subsetneq A$. Then $B\cup C\subseteq\Sbar$ and $A\setminus S\ne\emptyset$. Split $\Sbar$ into
  $A\setminus S$ and $B\cup C$; the crossing weight is
  $w(A\setminus S,B\cup C)\le w(A,B\cup C)=d(A)$.
\item $A$ and $S$ cross, i.e.\ $A\cap S$, $A\setminus S$, $S\setminus A$ and $V\setminus(A\cup S)$ are
  all nonempty. By posimodularity, $d(A)+d(S)\ge d(A\setminus S)+d(S\setminus A)$. Since
  $S\setminus A$ is a nonempty proper subset, $d(S\setminus A)\ge\lambda=d(S)$, so
  $d(A\setminus S)\le d(A)$. Split $\Sbar$ into $A\setminus S$ and $\Sbar\setminus A$, both nonempty;
  the crossing weight is at most $d(A\setminus S)\le d(A)$.
\end{enumerate}
For the second statement, $d(A)=w_{AB}+w_{AC}$, so $\lambda+d(A)\le W(A,B,C)$ exactly when
$\lambda\le w_{BC}$, and then $\greedy(S)\le\OPT\le\greedy(S)$.
\end{proof}

For the lightest part, cases (i) and (iv) essentially appear as Lemmas~3 and~2 of~\cite{KamidoiWY2002}.
The new ingredients are case~(iii) and the uniform pair-weight condition.
Theorem~\ref{thm:main} also follows from the $k$-cut argument of Section~\ref{sec:kcut} applied to
the pair $(B,C)$, since $W(A,B,C)-w_{BC}=d(A)$.

\begin{corollary}[weighted Hochbaum--Shmoys]\label{cor:hs}
Let $S$ be any global minimum-weight cut.
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*),leftmargin=*]
\item If some optimum has two parts $A,B$ joined by no edge, then $\greedy(S)=\OPT$.
\item If some optimum has a pair of parts joined by weight at least $\lambda$, then $\greedy(S)=\OPT$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
In (a), $w_{AC}=d(A)\ge\lambda$, so Theorem~\ref{thm:main} applies with the parts permuted; (b) is
Theorem~\ref{thm:main}.
\end{proof}

For unweighted planar graphs, (a) and (b) are Theorems~1 and~2 of~\cite{HochbaumShmoys1985}, stated
there for dual cycles; a theta-shaped dual with a strand at least as long as the girth corresponds
to~(b).

\begin{corollary}[the $4/3$ bound]\label{cor:43}
$\greedy(S)\le\tfrac43\,\OPT$ for every global minimum-weight cut $S$.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Choose $A$ opposite the heaviest pair, so $w_{BC}\ge\OPT/3$. Theorem~\ref{thm:main} gives
$\greedy(S)\le\max(\OPT+\lambda-w_{BC},\,\OPT)$, and
$\lambda\le\min(d(A),d(B),d(C))\le\tfrac23\OPT$.
\end{proof}

This recovers the tight bound of~\cite{HochbaumTsai1983}.

\begin{corollary}[running time]\label{cor:time}
The greedy algorithm needs one global minimum-weight cut of $G$ and one of each side; the sides are
edge-disjoint and inherit planarity or an embedding of genus $g$. It returns an optimum whenever some
optimum satisfies the criterion of Theorem~\ref{thm:main}, within the following times.
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{lll}
\toprule
Graph class & Global minimum-weight cut subroutine & Time\\
\midrule
General weighted & Henzinger--Li--Rao--Wang~\cite{HenzingerLRW2024} (deterministic) & near-linear\\
Planar & {\L}{\k{a}}cki--Sankowski~\cite{LackiSankowski2011} & $O(n\log\log n)$\\
Genus $g$ & Chambers--Erickson--Fox--Nayyeri~\cite{ChambersEFN} & $g^{O(g)}\,n\log\log n$\\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
\end{corollary}

\section{Hard instances}\label{sec:hard}

Call an optimum $(A,B,C)$ \emph{hard} if all three pair weights are below $\lambda$. By
Corollary~\ref{cor:hs}, greedy is exact unless every optimum is hard; so $\min(\greedy,H)$ is exact for
any procedure $H$ that always returns a valid 3-cut and is exact when all optima are hard.

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:hard}
Let $(A,B,C)$ be a hard optimum. Then
(1) $0<w_{XY}<\lambda$ for every pair of parts;
(2) $\lambda\le d(X)<2\lambda$ for every part $X$;
(3) $\tfrac32\lambda\le\OPT<3\lambda$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Each $d(X)\ge\lambda$ is a sum of two pair weights, each below $\lambda$, so both are positive and
$d(X)<2\lambda$. Summing, $2\,\OPT=\sum_X d(X)\ge3\lambda$, while $\OPT$, the sum of the three pair
weights, is below $3\lambda$.
\end{proof}

Every part of a hard optimum is thus a 2-approximate minimum-weight cut. In a plane graph, the dual of
a hard optimal cut is a theta-graph whose three strands are each lighter than the girth of the dual;
the other two dual shapes, two disjoint cycles or two cycles sharing a vertex, leave two parts with no
edges between them.

The next two lemmas enlarge a part of an optimum by a suitable minimum-weight cut without losing
optimality. Lemma~\ref{lem:merge-cut} is closely related to Xiao's Lemma~6~\cite{Xiao2008}, which gives
the corresponding non-crossing statement; the explicit merged optimum is what we use.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:merge-cut}
Let $(A,B,C)$ be an optimum, $a\in A$, and $R\subseteq V\setminus\{a\}$ a set meeting both $B$ and $C$.
If $X$ is the $a$-side of any minimum-weight cut separating $a$ from $R$, then
$(A\cup X,\,B\setminus X,\,C\setminus X)$ is an optimum.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The sets $A\cap X$, $X\setminus B$ and $X\setminus C$ each contain $a$ and avoid $R$, so each has cut
weight at least $d(X)$. Submodularity and posimodularity give
\begin{align*}
d(A)+d(X)&\ge d(A\cup X)+d(A\cap X),\\
d(B)+d(X)&\ge d(B\setminus X)+d(X\setminus B),\\
d(C)+d(X)&\ge d(C\setminus X)+d(X\setminus C).
\end{align*}
Summing, $2\,\OPT+3d(X)\ge 2\,W(A\cup X,B\setminus X,C\setminus X)+3d(X)$. The new parts are nonempty
because $R$ meets $B$ and $C$.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:merge-gh}
Let $(A,B,C)$ be an optimum and $T$ a Gomory--Hu tree. Let $e=(s,t)$ be a tree edge with $s\in A$, and
let $X$ be its Gomory--Hu side containing $s$. If $X$ contains neither all of $B$ nor all of $C$, then
$(A\cup X,\,B\setminus X,\,C\setminus X)$ is an optimum.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Every $Y\subseteq X$ with $s\in Y$ separates $s$ from $t$, so $d(Y)\ge d(X)$. The sets $A\cap X$,
$X\setminus B$ and $X\setminus C$ contain $s$ and lie in $X$, so the three inequalities of
Lemma~\ref{lem:merge-cut} apply. The new parts are nonempty by hypothesis.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}
If such an $X$ has at least two vertices, contracting $X$ preserves $\OPT$, since some optimum keeps
$X$ inside one part, and $T$ with $X$ contracted is a Gomory--Hu tree of $G/X$. Any statement of the
form ``some optimum has a part equal to a Gomory--Hu side'' therefore reduces, by induction on $n$, to
optima admitting no such merge. Section~\ref{sec:cex} shows that this residual case genuinely occurs.
\end{remark}

\section{Gomory--Hu sides do not suffice}\label{sec:cex}

Given a Gomory--Hu tree $T$, Kapoor's algorithm~\cite{Kapoor1996} first evaluates
\[
  F(T) \;=\; \min_{X}\Bigl(d(X)+\lambda\bigl(G[V\setminus X]\bigr)\Bigr),
\]
where $X$ ranges over the Gomory--Hu sides of $T$. It then recursively shrinks sides until $T$ is a
star, and handles that residual case with $O(n^3)$ max-flow computations. $F(T)$ is always the weight
of a 3-cut, so $F(T)\ge\OPT$, with equality whenever some optimum has a part that is a Gomory--Hu side.
Equality also holds whenever greedy is exact, in particular under the criterion of
Theorem~\ref{thm:main}: the side $S$ of a lightest tree edge is a global minimum-weight cut, and
$F(T)\le\greedy(S)$. One might hope that equality always holds, which would make
the recursion unnecessary. It does not.

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:cex}
There is a weighted graph on 7 vertices with a unique Gomory--Hu tree $T$ and a unique optimum, such
that no part of the optimum is a side of $T$ and $F(T)>\OPT$.
\end{theorem}

\paragraph{The instance.}
Vertices $s,u_0,\ell_0,\ell_1,\ell_2,c_0,c_1$ and edge weights:

$c_0\ell_0$ & 0.166989 & $sc_0$ & 0.035930\\
$c_1\ell_0$ & 0.035652 & $s\ell_1$ & 0.210285\\
$\ell_1\ell_0$ & 0.221789 & $s\ell_2$ & 0.111054\\
$\ell_1\ell_2$ & 0.194170 & $su_0$ & 0.364863\\

\end{center}

\paragraph{Verification.}
All quantities below are for the weights exactly as printed. They were computed by exhaustive
enumeration in exact integer arithmetic on the weights $10^6w$: cut values over all 126 nonempty
proper vertex subsets, the optimum over all 3-partitions, and the Gomory--Hu tree over all
$7^5=16{,}807$ labelled spanning trees. Every item has also been verified in the Lean~4 proof
assistant, by kernel evaluation of the corresponding finite statements (no compiled code is trusted);
there, uniqueness of the tree is checked over all $7^6$ parent arrays rooted at $s$, which include
every spanning tree. Both verifications are provided as supplementary material.
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
\item \emph{The tree.} $T$ is the caterpillar with edges $s\ell_0$ (0.631143), $s\ell_1$ (0.626244),
  $s\ell_2$ (0.622248), $su_0$ (0.713745), $u_0c_0$ (0.685567) and $u_0c_1$ (0.699878). It is the only
  spanning tree with the cut-tree property, and the minimum-weight cut of each of its edges is unique.
\item \emph{The optimum.} It is unique: $(\{s,u_0\},\{\ell_0,\ell_1,\ell_2\},\{c_0,c_1\})$, with
  $\OPT=1.032456$.
\item \emph{Hardness.} $\lambda=0.622248$ and the pair weights are $0.312952$, $0.398165$ and
  $0.321339$, so the optimum is hard and greedy is not exact.
\item \emph{The gap.} The sides of $T$ are the five single leaves, their complements,
  $\{s,\ell_0,\ell_1,\ell_2\}$ and its complement $\{u_0,c_0,c_1\}$. Completing each optimally gives
  $F(T)=1.035084>\OPT$.
\end{itemize}
The instance is a random $5\%$ perturbation of a vertex of a linear program, so the failure is not an
artefact of ties.

\paragraph{How the instance was found.}
By Lemma~\ref{lem:merge-gh}, $F(T)$ can exceed $\OPT$ only in a graph none of whose optima admits a
merge of a Gomory--Hu side with at least two vertices. This restricts how the parts can sit in $T$.
Guided by it, we searched caterpillar trees: a spine of vertices of a part $A$, with a part $B$ as the
leaves at one end of the spine and a part $C$ as the leaves at the other. For a fixed tree shape, the condition that $T$ be a
Gomory--Hu tree is linear in the edge weights: every cut separating the endpoints of a tree edge weighs
at least that edge's fundamental cut. A linear program then maximizes the margin by which every
3-partition with a Gomory--Hu-side part loses to $(A,B,C)$. The margin is $0$ for $|B|=1$, for
$|B|=2$ with $|C|\in\{1,2\}$, and for $(|B|,|C|)=(3,1)$; it is positive for $(|B|,|C|)=(3,2)$, which is
the instance above.

\section{Minimum $k$-cut}\label{sec:kcut}

For $k\ge2$, $W(P)$ denotes the weight of the edges crossing a $k$-partition $P$, and $\OPT_k$ is its
minimum. A partition $P$ is \emph{compatible} with a cut $S$ if each of its parts lies inside $S$ or
inside $\Sbar=V\setminus S$. For a global minimum-weight cut $S$ let
\[
  R_k(S) \;=\; \lambda + \min_{1\le j\le k-1}\Bigl(\OPT_j\bigl(G[S]\bigr)+\OPT_{k-j}\bigl(G[\Sbar]\bigr)\Bigr),
\]
with $\OPT_1=0$ and $\OPT_j(H)=\infty$ if $H$ has fewer than $j$ vertices. This is the weight of a
cheapest $k$-partition compatible with $S$, and $R_3(S)=\greedy(S)$.

Compatibility is stronger than Xiao's notion of a cut being \emph{non-crossing} with a
$k$-partition~\cite[Definition~2]{Xiao2008}, which asks only that one side of the cut lie inside a
part. Xiao showed that every global minimum-weight cut is non-crossing with some minimum-weight
3-partition, and that this fails for $k\ge4$~\cite[Lemmas~5 and~9]{Xiao2008}. Compatibility can fail
already for $k=3$: since $R_3(S)=\greedy(S)$, it fails exactly when greedy is not exact, as for the
graph of Theorem~\ref{thm:cex}. A small example for $k=4$: take a 4-cycle with unit weights and a
pendant vertex $p$ attached by an edge of weight $2$. Then $\lambda=2$, attained by $\{p\}$, and
$\OPT_4=4$, by cutting the four cycle edges. But a 4-partition compatible with $\{p\}$ has $\{p\}$ as a
part and cuts the cycle into three pieces, so $R_4(\{p\})=5>\OPT_4$.

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:kcut}
Let $k\ge3$, let $P$ be a $k$-partition with parts $A_i\ne A_j$, and let $S$ be a global minimum-weight
cut. Then
\[
  R_k(S)\;\le\;\max\bigl(W(P)-w(A_i,A_j)+\lambda,\;W(P)\bigr).
\]
In particular, if some minimum-weight $k$-partition has two parts joined by weight at least $\lambda$,
then $R_k(S)=\OPT_k$ for every global minimum-weight cut $S$; that is, every global minimum-weight cut
is compatible with some minimum-weight $k$-partition.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Merge $A_i$ and $A_j$ to obtain a $(k-1)$-partition $P'$ with $W(P')=W(P)-w(A_i,A_j)$, and refine $P'$
by $S$, replacing each part $X$ by its nonempty pieces among $X\cap S$ and $X\cap\Sbar$. Every edge
crossing the refinement crosses $P'$ or $S$, so the refinement weighs at most $W(P')+\lambda$.

If $S$ splits some part of $P'$, the refinement has at least $k$ pieces. Merging pieces that lie on
the same side of $S$ keeps every piece inside $S$ or $\Sbar$, does not increase the weight, and can
bring the number of pieces down to exactly $k$. This gives a compatible $k$-partition of weight at most
$W(P)-w(A_i,A_j)+\lambda$.

Otherwise every part of $P'$ lies inside $S$ or $\Sbar$. In particular $A_i\cup A_j$ does, so $P$
itself is compatible with $S$, and $R_k(S)\le W(P)$. The second statement follows by taking $P$ optimal
with $w(A_i,A_j)\ge\lambda$.
\end{proof}

Refining a partition by a cut and coarsening the result is a standard device in the analysis of
$k$-cuts; see, for instance, Lemma~5 of Vaughn~\cite{Vaughn2026} and the uncrossing arguments of
Kamidoi, Yoshida and Nagamochi~\cite{KamidoiYoshidaNagamochi2007} and Xiao~\cite{Xiao2008kway}.
What Theorem~\ref{thm:kcut} adds is the observation that a single pair of parts joined by weight at
least $\lambda$ pays for the refinement.

For $k=3$ and the pair $(B,C)$, $W(P)-w_{BC}+\lambda=\lambda+d(A)$, which is
Theorem~\ref{thm:main}.

\begin{corollary}\label{cor:kapprox}
For every $k\ge3$ and every global minimum-weight cut $S$,
\[
  R_k(S)\;\le\;\Bigl(1+\frac{2(k-2)}{k(k-1)}\Bigr)\OPT_k .
\]
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Let $P$ be optimal and $(A_i,A_j)$ its heaviest pair, so $w(A_i,A_j)\ge\OPT_k/\binom k2$. Also
$\lambda\le\min_i d(A_i)\le 2\,\OPT_k/k$, because $\sum_i d(A_i)=2\,\OPT_k$. Theorem~\ref{thm:kcut} gives
$R_k(S)\le\OPT_k+\max\bigl(0,\,2\OPT_k/k-2\OPT_k/(k(k-1))\bigr)$.
\end{proof}

The factor is $4/3$ for $k=3$ and $k=4$, $13/10$ for $k=5$, and tends to $1$. It concerns the best
compatible partition rather than an algorithm, since $R_k$ requires exact solutions on the two sides.

\begin{corollary}\label{cor:khard}
If $R_k(S)>\OPT_k$ for some global minimum-weight cut $S$, then every minimum-weight $k$-partition has
all $\binom k2$ pair weights below $\lambda$, and $k\lambda/2\le\OPT_k<\binom k2\lambda$.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
The first claim is the contrapositive of Theorem~\ref{thm:kcut}. Then
$\OPT_k=\tfrac12\sum_i d(A_i)\ge k\lambda/2$, and $\OPT_k$, a sum of $\binom k2$ pair weights each below
$\lambda$, is below $\binom k2\lambda$.
\end{proof}

In the 5-vertex example above, the pair weights of the optimum are $1$ or $0$, all below $\lambda=2$,
as Corollary~\ref{cor:khard} requires.

\section{Computational checks}\label{sec:comp}

The statements above were tested against brute-force optima on random graphs: planar graphs
(Delaunay triangulations of random points, each edge kept with probability $0.8$) and $G(n,0.45)$
graphs with 5--11 vertices, with weights uniform in $[1,10]$ or uniform integers $1,\dots,5$; and, for
$F(T)$, near-complete, circulant, wheel and three-cluster graphs with 7--10 vertices. The adversarial
search hill-climbs on the weights of random graphs with 11--13 vertices to maximize $F(T)$ minus the
least weight of a 3-partition with no Gomory--Hu side as a part.
All quantities were computed by exhaustive enumeration over vertex subsets and partitions, and every
Gomory--Hu tree was rebuilt from exhaustively computed minimum-weight cuts and re-verified. Each row
is reproduced by one of the supplementary scripts.
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{p{0.6\textwidth}rr}
\toprule
Statement & Instances & Violations\\
\midrule

Corollary~\ref{cor:hs}: if greedy is inexact, every optimum is hard & 121 graphs & 0\\

$F(T)=\OPT$, structured graphs & 1{,}577 graphs & 0\\

Violations of $F(T)=\OPT$ are thus rare and structured: none appeared in random or locally optimized
instances, only in instances built from the caterpillar program of Section~\ref{sec:cex}. The last
row is expected to fail: it shows that the hypothesis of Theorem~\ref{thm:kcut} cannot be weakened
to ``the parts other than one are joined among themselves by weight at least $\lambda$''.

\paragraph{A numerical caveat.}
Gomory--Hu trees must be built with care in floating point. In early experiments a widely used library
routine for minimum $s$--$t$ cuts occasionally returned a source side inconsistent with the flow value,
because residual capacities of order $10^{-15}$ were treated as usable; the resulting trees were
flow-equivalent but not cut-equivalent, and an adversarial search exploited them to produce false
counterexamples. The supplementary scripts therefore avoid max-flow routines altogether and verify
every tree they use.

\section{Open questions}

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*]
\item \emph{A small candidate family for hard instances.} Nagamochi and Ibaraki~\cite{NagamochiIbaraki2000}
  prove that some optimum has a part among $O(n)$ cuts listed in nondecreasing order up to the first
  crossing pair. Can an equally small family be read off a Gomory--Hu tree alone? Theorem~\ref{thm:cex}
  rules out its sides. Also trying as a part, for every tree vertex, the set of its leaf neighbours
  closes the gap in one of the four failures of Section~\ref{sec:comp} but not in the other three.
\item \emph{Hard instances in near-linear time.} Outside the hard class, weighted minimum 3-cut is
  near-linear on planar and bounded-genus graphs (Corollary~\ref{cor:time}); in general graphs Vaughn's
  $\tilde O(n^2)$ is conditionally optimal. In a plane graph $G$ with dual $H$,
  $\OPT(G)=\min(\greedy,\mathrm{MinTheta}(H))$, where $\mathrm{MinTheta}$ is the least weight of a
  theta subgraph, since every plane theta is 3-separating and non-theta optima are found by greedy
  (Corollary~\ref{cor:hs}(a)). So the question asks for a theta-analogue of the near-linear planar
  girth algorithms. Can the minimum light theta of a plane graph, one whose three strands are each
  lighter than the girth of $H$, be found in $o(n^2)$ time?
\item \emph{Larger $k$, algorithmically.} Theorem~\ref{thm:kcut} settles when a global minimum-weight
  cut is compatible with an optimal $k$-partition, but exploiting this needs exact $j$-cuts of the two
  sides, for which the condition need not hold again. Is there a condition under which the whole
  recursion, with a global minimum-weight cut at every level, is exact?
\item \emph{Hochbaum--Tsai.} Their manuscript~\cite{HochbaumTsai1983} proving the $4/3$ bound is cited
  in~\cite{HochbaumShmoys1985} but appears unavailable; it may contain parts of Theorem~\ref{thm:main}.
\end{enumerate}
